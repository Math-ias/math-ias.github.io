I read “Franklin Pierce” by Michael F. Holt.

This book was mechanically much better than the book in the “The American Presidents” series. My only complaint was that it was a little short: 133 paperback-sized pages.

I was intrigued by this complex character. Pierce was …

  • a party boy who loved the outdoors, but married a feeble shut-in.
  • he was from New Hampshire and yet bucked the trend of the North, and advocated on behalf of the expansion of slavery into the territories (a political mistake that cost him and his party dearly).
  • He was proud of his family’s patriotism in the revolutionary and 1812 wars, and was a member of Jackson’s pro-democracy party, and yet flirted with Know-Nothings who wanted to reduce new Catholic (from so many immigrants from Catholic countries like Ireland) voting power.
  • He sought the union of the Democratic Party as a high ideal worth politicking for, and yet wasn’t a strong proponent of keeping the greater Union of the United States together during the civil war (he only supported defending against Southern aggression).
  • He liked going to church, but had to shop around until he found a place that didn’t lambast the immorality of slavery.

I thought it was interesting how much success the Democratic Party was having. Across the north there were plenty of people who were fearful of upsetting the status quo. That is until Kansas. Pro-slavery factions wanted their cake and to eat it too (having slave states above and below the 36°30′ latitude line). I view this as a moral warning. If evil is left to fester, it only grows and takes more and more concessions.
